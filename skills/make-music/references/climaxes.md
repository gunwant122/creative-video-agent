# Building climaxes

A climax works because of what comes before it. Plan backwards from the payoff moment.

## Levers (add them one at a time, not all at once)
1. **Register** - widen: bass goes lower, melody goes higher.
2. **Density** - more instruments, then more notes per bar.
3. **Rhythm** - long notes -> quarters -> 8ths ostinato.
4. **Dynamics** - crescendo across 4-8 bars, velocity rising steadily.
5. **Harmony** - faster chord changes; a pedal that finally resolves.
6. **New colour** - the instrument you held back (horns, timpani, cymbals) enters at the peak.

## Shape
- Build over 10-30 s for a short piece. Too short feels abrupt; too long loses tension.
- **Pull back just before the peak**: a beat or two of silence or near-silence, then the hit. The drop makes the arrival louder.
- The peak lands on a picture moment from the cue sheet (the reveal, the final shot) - exactly.
- After the peak: one sustained resolution chord, decaying through the last shot or into black. Don't end on the hit unless the picture cuts to black on it.

## Final statement
The motif's full, major or most confident version arrives at the peak, played by the "heroic" colour. If it has already been heard in full that way, the climax will feel flat - hold it back.
