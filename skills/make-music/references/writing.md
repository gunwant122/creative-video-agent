# Writing: motif, harmony, development

## One motif, developed
- Write **one** main motif of 3-6 notes with a recognisable rhythm or interval (a rising sixth, a falling step pattern). A short score needs one idea, not three.
- Develop it across sections instead of writing new tunes:
  - **Fragment** - first 2-3 notes only, under tension
  - **Augment** - slower, long notes, for grief or awe
  - **Re-harmonise** - same notes over new chords to change meaning
  - **Re-orchestrate** - fragile on harp/solo violin, heroic on horns
  - **Mode change** - minor statement early, major (or Picardy third) at the payoff
- The full, confident statement comes once - at the emotional peak.

## Choosing chords for emotion (minor key, e.g. D minor)
| Feeling | Progression (in D minor) |
|---|---|
| Sorrow, fragile | i - VI - iv - i (Dm - Bb - Gm - Dm) |
| Hope rising | i - VI - III - VII (Dm - Bb - F - C) |
| Tension, threat | i - bII (Dm - Eb), pedal on D with shifting chords above |
| Resolve, triumph | iv - V - i, or VI - VII - I (Bb - C - D major) |
| Bittersweet | VI - VII - i with a suspended 4th resolving late |

- Pedal tones (a held low note under changing chords) give tension cheaply.
- Voice-lead smoothly: move each inner voice by step or not at all.

## Sections
Write each scene's cue as a section with its own palette and tempo, connected by the motif. Note them in `work/score_notes.md`:
