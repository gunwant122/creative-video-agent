# Cinematic / orchestral scoring

Suits period drama, emotional shorts, epic and historical stories.

## Palette by mood
| Mood | Instruments | Texture |
|---|---|---|
| Fragile, intimate, illness | Harp arpeggios, solo violin or cello, soft high strings | Sparse, long reverb |
| Hope, rising | Violins in octaves, warm low strings | Theme in long notes, swelling |
| Authority, conflict, council | Cello/bass ostinato (repeated 8ths), horns, low brass | Driving pulse, minor, pedal |
| Sacred, fate, church | Tubular bells, choir-like string chords, organ-like sustains | Slow, open fifths |
| Suspense, heartbeat | Pizzicato low strings, timpani soft rolls | Steady pulse at ~60-70 bpm |
| Triumph, finale | Theme on horns, full strings, timpani, cymbal | Full range, major or Picardy third |

## Orchestration rules
- Spread chords: wide at the bottom (octaves/fifths below C3), closer at the top.
- Double the melody an octave apart (violins + violas, or horns + cellos) for weight.
- Horns are the "heroic" colour; use them for the payoff, not throughout.
- Harp is decorative - arpeggios and glissandi, not sustained harmony.
- Keep 1-2 instruments silent until late so the climax has something new.
- Sampled libraries sound fake on fast passages and exposed solos. Prefer long notes, legato lines, ostinati and doubled parts.

## Mixing the orchestra stems
- Strings and harp: one reverb, longish (2-3 s hall).
- Percussion hits: slightly less reverb so they stay punchy on the cut.
- Pan like a stage: violins left, cellos right-centre, basses right, horns centre-left, harp left, percussion centre-back.
